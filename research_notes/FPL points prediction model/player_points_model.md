# Player-level FPL expected points: components and current (2026/27) scoring rules

Research date: 25 October 2026 (mid 2026/27). The official rules page (fantasy.premierleague.com/help/rules)
is a JavaScript app and returned no content to the fetcher. Rules below come from official premierleague.com
articles dated July 2026 (2026/27) where possible. Anything taken from a 2025/26-dated source is marked [2025/26 source].

## 1. Exact current FPL scoring rules (2026/27) and changes since 2024/25

### Takeaway
Base FPL points have been unchanged since 2025/26. That season added defensive contributions (DefCon: 2 pts at
10 CBIT for defenders, 12 CBIRT for midfielders and forwards) and a looser assist definition. 2026/27 changed only
the BPS, in three ways: no -1 for being tackled, 1 BPS per 3 CBI (was per 2), and a reworked goalkeeper save
table with +1 for saving a big chance. Lockdown also moves to 09:00 UK the day after the gameweek, which matters
because bonus and DefCon can be corrected until then.

### Cited Findings
**Points table, 2026/27** (official "FPL basics explained: Scoring points", The Scout, 20 July 2026) — [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174909)

| Action | GK | DEF | MID | FWD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Played 1 to 59 min | +1 | +1 | +1 | +1 |
| Played 60+ min (excl. stoppage time) | +2 | +2 | +2 | +2 |
| Goal | +10 | +6 | +5 | +4 |
| Assist | +3 | +3 | +3 | +3 |
| Clean sheet (needs 60+ min) | +4 | +4 | +1 | 0 |
| Every 3 saves | +1 | – | – | – |
| Penalty save | +5 | – | – | – |
| Penalty miss | -2 | -2 | -2 | -2 |
| DefCon: 10 CBIT | – | +2 | – | – |
| DefCon: 12 CBIRT | – | – | +2 | +2 |
| Every 2 goals conceded | -1 | -1 | – | – |
| Yellow / red card | -1 / -3 | same | same | same |
| Own goal | -2 | -2 | -2 | -2 |
| Bonus (BPS rank in match) | 3 / 2 / 1 | same | same | same |

- The appearance thresholds are 1 point for playing up to 60 minutes and 2 for 60 or more, excluding stoppage
  time. A player subbed off after 60+ minutes keeps the clean sheet if no goal had been conceded by then. Tied
  players share a bonus place, so more than three can earn bonus — [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174909)
- On goalkeeper goals: one guide says a GK goal is 10 and that 6 is often misreported. The official 2026/27
  table says 10 — [FFS search summary/Fix](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-2026-27-new-rules/); [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174909)
- DefCon (new in 2025/26) is capped at 2 points per match. CBIT counts clearances, blocks, interceptions and
  tackles. Midfielders and forwards also count ball recoveries (CBIRT) — [PL 2025/26](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4362211); [Fix 2025/26](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-2025-26-new-rules/)
- DefCon is unchanged for 2026/27 ("no threshold changes"). Calls to double-reward 20 contributions were not
  taken up — [FFS 20 Jul 2026](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/07/20/fpl-2026-27-5-rule-changes-new-features-announced). One blog, Onside, says the full-back threshold is "under review" but gives no figures. Treat that as unconfirmed — [Onside](https://onsidearena.com/tips/fpl-rule-changes-2026-27)

**Assists (expanded in 2025/26, still current)** — [PL assists article](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4362187)
- An assist is the scoring team's final touch before the goal. The final touch can be "a pass, an inadvertent
  touch, an effort on goal or winning a penalty or free-kick".
- Inside the box, the intended target of the pass no longer matters, provided there is at most one defensive
  touch (not a pass) and possession isn't lost and regained. Outside the box, the scorer must still be the
  intended target.
- Handball: the "forced" element is dropped. An attacking action that leads to a defensive handball and a scored
  penalty or free kick earns an assist, unless another defender touched the ball first.
- Defensive errors: only a misplaced defender pass now blocks an assist (previously any unforced error did).
- PL estimate: the new rules would have produced 41 more assists in 2024/25.
- Not stated in the article: rebounds from saved shots and own-goal assists. The "effort on goal" wording implies
  a saved or blocked shot that is put in can count.

**BPS table** (PL "How the FPL Bonus Points System works", dated 1 Apr 2026 [2025/26 values]) — [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/106533)
- Positive: play 1–60 min 3; 60+ min 6; penalty goal 12; GK/DEF goal 12; MID goal 18; FWD goal 24; assist 9;
  GK/DEF clean sheet 12; penalty save 8; save inside box 3; save outside box 2; successful open-play cross 1;
  big chance created 3; every 2 CBI 1; every 3 recoveries 1; key pass 1; successful tackle 2; successful
  dribble 1; winning goal 3; goal-line clearance 9; foul won 1; shot on target 2; pass completion (30+
  attempts): 70–79% 2, 80–89% 4, 90%+ 6.
- Negative: GK/DEF goal conceded -4; penalty conceded -3; penalty miss -6; yellow -3; red -9; own goal -6;
  big chance missed -3; error leading to goal -3; error leading to attempt -1; being tackled -1; foul conceded
  -1; offside -1; shot off target -1.
- Ties: two tied for 1st both get 3 and the next player gets 1. Two tied for 2nd both get 2. Two tied for 3rd
  both get 1.

**2026/27 BPS changes** — [FFS](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/07/20/fpl-2026-27-5-rule-changes-new-features-announced); [Fix](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-2026-27-new-rules/); [PL 2026/27 changes](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4679873/all-you-need-to-know-about-changes-to-fpl-for-202627)
- "Being tackled -1" is removed. Fix words this as "successful tackle" deduction removed, which looks like a
  wording slip. FFS says "when tackled".
- CBI: 1 BPS per 3 (was per 2).
- GK saves: 2 BPS for any save, +1 if inside the box (so 3), +1 for saving a big chance. The separate
  outside-box metric is gone. Penalty save drops from 8 to 7 BPS, plus 1 for the big-chance save (net 8).
- PL's stated aims: reduce overlap with DefCon and improve bonus chances for GKs, full-backs and attackers.
  PL gives no numbers itself. The figures come from FFS and Fix.
- Other 2026/27 changes: projected bonus added live after 20 minutes. Lockdown at 09:00 UK the day after the
  gameweek's last match, so Opta can correct bonus and DefCon. 11 players reclassified to new positions (not
  listed in the PL article). No extra AFCON free transfers. Chips are still 2 sets of 4, the first set used
  before the GW19 deadline. Free transfers still roll to a maximum of 5 — [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4679873/all-you-need-to-know-about-changes-to-fpl-for-202627)
- 2025/26 vs 2024/25 (beyond DefCon and assists): BPS values for GK saves, goal-line clearances, penalty goals
  and successful tackles changed. That article gives no numbers. There is no Assistant Manager chip — [PL 2025/26](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4362211)

### Inferences
- A 2026/27 BPS simulator should start from the 1 Apr 2026 table and apply the 2026/27 tweaks:
  - Drop "being tackled".
  - CBI: floor(CBI/3) instead of floor(CBI/2).
  - GK saves: 2·saves + 1·inside-box saves + 1·big-chance saves.
  - Penalty save: 7.
- Net effect: centre-backs lose BPS, while goalkeepers and dribblers gain.
- Re-check the reclassified players' positions in the FPL API (`element_type`) rather than relying on articles.

### Gaps
- I could not render the official rules page, so the 2026/27 BPS values come from FFS and Fix, not a PL table.
- I could not confirm whether the penalty-goal BPS (12) or the tackle BPS (2) changed in 2025/26 from older values.
- I could not confirm whether the GK goal value was 6 or 10 in 2024/25, so whether it changed is unknown.
- The names of the 11 reclassified players weren't retrieved.

## 2. Minutes and appearance prediction

### Takeaway
Minutes are the gating variable for every component. Good models split it into P(start), the minutes
distribution given a start, and the chance of a sub appearance, all built from recent match logs with decay.
They then override with flags and team news. FPL Review's edge over public-data models is mostly on low
returns (0s and 1s), which the OpenFPL paper links to minutes data.

### Cited Findings
- FPL Review xMins is the average over 1,000 simulations that include rotation, injury risk, tactics and news.
  It decays over the horizon based on injury proneness and how securely the player owns his role. It is
  refreshed hourly, checked by humans after matches, press conferences and leaks, and re-run at the deadline.
  They stress "EV is not directly proportional to xMins" — [FPL Review docs](https://docs.fplreview.com/the-model/projections/xmins/)
- AIrsenal's RecentMinutesModel takes the minutes from a player's last N matches (at least 3) as equally likely
  scenarios. Gaps are filled with last season's average for his current team. A chance of playing of 50% or
  less sets minutes to 0. Points are computed per scenario and averaged — [AIrsenal how-it-works](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- fpl-machine (open source):
  - P(start): prior-season start rate, shrunk toward a low prior when minutes are few, with current-season
    starts taking over as the season goes on.
  - Overrides: the chance-of-playing flag replaces P(start), and injury flags zero it.
  - P(60+ | start) is logistic in minutes per start. A player averaging 64 minutes per start is far more often
    hooked before 60 than one averaging 88.
  - [kelihh/fpl-machine](https://github.com/kelihh/fpl-machine)
- In Gaffer PR #30, replacing season-total ÷ rounds with a decayed per-gameweek log, split into P(start) and
  minutes given start or sub, cut the minutes error metric by about 23%. The gain held at 1, 2 and 3 gameweeks
  ahead — [GitHub PR](https://github.com/M9chaelangelo/Gaffer/pull/30)
- After a confirmed start, rate terms (per-90 rates, clean-sheet eligibility) must be re-conditioned too, not
  just minutes. Otherwise projections are biased for exactly the rotation-risk players whose news matters —
  [best-ball-pl issue #216](https://github.com/wulke/best-ball-pl/issues/216)
- OpenFPL uses only FPL API availability tags (0/25/50/75/100%) and no expected-minutes data. FPL Review has
  lower RMSE on Zeros (0.689 vs 0.818) and Blanks (1.189 vs 1.291). OpenFPL is better on Tickers (1.517 vs 1.594)
  and Haulers (5.142 vs 5.172). The author attributes FPL Review's low-return edge to start/sub/rest information
  the API lacks. This is an interpretation, not a measured error decomposition — [OpenFPL arXiv 2508.09992](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09992v1)

### Inferences
- Structure:
  - P(app) = P(start) + (1 − P(start))·P(sub)
  - P(60+) = P(start)·P(60+ | start) + (1 − P(start))·P(sub before 30')
  - E[appearance] = 1·P(1–59) + 2·P(60+)
- Inputs: the FPL `chance_of_playing_next_round` flag (as a cap or override), press-conference lineups,
  European midweek fixtures (rotation, as a fixture-congestion covariate), and new signings (a prior from
  the previous club's role, shrunk).
- Simulate minutes jointly per team (11 starters, at most 5 subs) to keep the totals consistent.

### Gaps
- I found no published quantitative split of total xPts error into minutes vs per-90 performance. OpenFPL's
  claim is qualitative.
- I found no sourced numbers for how European congestion affects start probability.

## 3. Player share of team attack (goals, assists), shrinkage, finishing, role changes

### Takeaway
The standard approach takes team expected goals from a team model and allocates goals and assists by the
player's share of team xG and xA. The shares are Bayesian-shrunk (Dirichlet or multinomial) toward position
priors, and scaled by expected minutes. Finishing over xG is weak and noisy, so shrink it hard. Penalty takers
get a separate penalty-xG term.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal:
  - Team model: xG-based ratings with a Conway-Maxwell-Poisson goals distribution.
  - Player model: a Dirichlet over each player's share of team xG and xA (`conjugate` variant: actual scorers
    and assisters).
  - Attacking points: enumerate the splits of the team's goals into player goals, player assists and others,
    weight by the shares, scale by the fraction of the match played, and multiply by position points.
  - [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- Finishing skill (Bayesian hierarchical logistic, xG as the baseline, partial pooling over 7,500+ players
  and 5 leagues, 2014/15–2025/26):
  - Top-10 finishing skill is about +0.23 to +0.35 log-odds, and the bottom 10 about −0.14 to −0.20.
  - Credible intervals are wide: Son 0.19–0.54, Messi 0.15–0.43.
  - +0.29 turns a 10% xG shot into about 13%, roughly 3 extra goals per 100 shots.
  - [pena.lt](https://pena.lt/y/2025/10/01/a-better-way-to-measure-finishing-skill/)
- Messi beat his xG by only about 25% over his career. A 25%-better finisher on 100 shots a season beats xG in
  at least 4 of 5 seasons only 70% of the time. Shot creation, not finishing, is the main repeatable skill —
  [arXiv 2401.09940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.09940); [arXiv 2512.00203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00203)

### Inferences
- Formulas:
  - λ_team = team xG for the fixture.
  - Player goals ~ Poisson(λ_team · s_g · m/90), where s_g is the shrunk share of team non-penalty xG per 90
    on the pitch.
  - Add the penalty term: P(pen awarded) · P(on pitch and on pens) · about 0.78 conversion.
  - Assists are analogous with s_a (xA share). The 2025/26 assist definition raises the base rate for shooters,
    since rebounds count, and for penalty and free-kick winners.
- Shrinkage: s = (n·ŝ + k·s_prior)/(n + k), with n = minutes/90 and s_prior from position, price or role.
  Equivalently, use a Beta or Dirichlet prior.
- Finishing multiplier: shrink to about 0 unless the player has several seasons. At most about +30% on shot
  xG for elite finishers.
- Transfers and role changes: carry the share prior across, but rescale by the new team's xG and the role's
  share, and weight new-club minutes more heavily.

### Gaps
- No sourced penalty conversion rate was retrieved; 0.76–0.79 is a commonly cited figure but unverified here.
- No published values for the prior strength k were found.

## 4. Defensive contribution (DefCon) probability

### Takeaway
DefCon is a threshold event, so what matters is P(count ≥ 10 or 12), not the mean. Model the CBIT or CBIRT
count with a Poisson or negative binomial whose rate scales with minutes, opponent possession and game state.
Centre-backs and defensive midfielders in low-possession teams dominate.

### Cited Findings
- One open-source implementation shrinks CBIT per 90 toward a prior and takes P(≥ threshold) from a Poisson.
  It found match counts close to Poisson (variance/mean about 1.02 for DEF, 0.81 for MID, so midfielders are
  under-dispersed). Predicted hits summed close to actual hits. Overall error was unchanged on GW2–5 holdout
  (MAE 1.1167 to 1.1173). This is thin evidence from a personal project — [GitHub PR #57](https://github.com/amanwaseem/fpl-predictor/pull/57)
- 2024/25 retro-applied rates:
  - Tarkowski hit 10 CBIT in 22 games; Huijsen had the best rate, 73.1% of starts.
  - Casemiro hit in 66.7% of starts and Caicedo in 55.3%, Wieffer 60%.
  - The top 10 of each list were all centre-backs and defensive midfielders.
  - Low-possession teams (Everton 57 defender hits, Forest 50) produce more hits. Man City, Villa, Arsenal and
    Chelsea had the fewest.
  - [The Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/articles/fpl-defensive-contributions-2025-26-best-picks)

### Inferences
- E[DefCon] = 2 · Σ_m P(m) · P(N ≥ T | rate·m/90). Here T = 10 for DEF and 12 for MID/FWD.
- The rate is multiplied by the opponent's possession share (or 1 − own expected possession) and adjusted for
  game state: a leading team defends more.
- Under-dispersion in midfielders means Poisson over-states tail mass. A Conway-Maxwell-Poisson or binomial is
  a better fit. Check the players reclassified for 2026/27, since a forward-turned-midfielder keeps the 12
  threshold but a midfielder-turned-defender gets 10 and can drop recoveries.

### Gaps
- No published per-90 CBIT/CBIRT averages or game-state elasticities were found.

## 5. Clean sheets and goals conceded

### Takeaway
Use the team model's goals-against distribution. A clean sheet needs the player on the pitch for 60+ minutes and
no goal while he is on. The goals-conceded deduction counts only goals conceded while he is on.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal awards clean sheet points only to players expected to play 60+ minutes: 4·P(team concedes 0) for
  GK and DEF, 1·P(0) for MID. Goals conceded are −1 per 2, scaled by the fraction of the match played —
  [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- A player subbed after 60+ minutes keeps the clean sheet if no goal had been conceded while he was on —
  [PL](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174909)

### Inferences
- With μ = team xGA and minutes m ≥ 60: P(CS) ≈ exp(−μ·m/90). For a sub-off at minute m, use μ·m/90. A late
  goal after he leaves doesn't cost him.
- E[GC penalty] = −Σ_k floor(k/2)·P(K = k), with K ~ Poisson(μ·m/90).

### Gaps
- None material.

## 6. Goalkeeper saves

### Takeaway
Saves ≈ opponent shots on target minus goals. Points are floor(saves/3). Model expected opponent SoT from the
team model, then use a Poisson or binomial to get P(saves ≥ 3k). 2026/27 BPS also rewards big-chance saves.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal predicts saves (along with bonus, cards and DefCon) as the player's past average shrunk toward the
  positional average — [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- Goalkeepers have a high BPS floor and a low ceiling, so their BPS converts to bonus poorly —
  [search summary of allaboutfpl/FFS](https://allaboutfpl.com/2022/07/what-is-bps-in-fpl-which-players-benefit-more-from-bps/)

### Inferences
- With S ~ Poisson(opp SoT), saves = S − goals. Simulating jointly with goals conceded gives the right negative
  correlation between saves and clean sheets.

### Gaps
- No sourced penalty-save rate.

## 7. Bonus points

### Takeaway
Bonus depends on a player's BPS rank within his match. Simulate every player's BPS from simulated events, then
rank with the official tie rules. Cheaper alternatives are a logistic regression on features or a shrunk
historical bonus-per-90.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal: shrunk average bonus — [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- Phelps (2018) predicted bonus with logistic regression on BPS plus position, home/away and team —
  [blog](https://jonathan-phelps.netlify.com/2018/08/fpl-bonus-points/)
- Rough historical cut-offs: about 42 BPS for 3 bonus, 33 for 2, 28 for 1 (older pre-2025 era) —
  [FourFourTwo](https://www.fourfourtwo.com/news/fpl-tips-predicting-where-your-bonus-points-can-come-from-1605700382000)
- The 2026/27 changes cut centre-back BPS (CBI per 3, and the earlier tackle changes) and raise GK BPS —
  [FFS](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/07/20/fpl-2026-27-5-rule-changes-new-features-announced)

### Inferences
- Simulation: for each match, draw goals, assists, clean sheet and saves from the shared team simulation. Add a
  baseline BPS per 90 for each player (passing, key passes, recoveries, and so on, from history and re-scored
  under 2026/27 rules). Rank, then apply ties.
- Re-calibrate the cut-offs on 2025/26 and 2026/27 data, since the rule changes shift BPS levels.

### Gaps
- No public 2026/27 bonus cut-off statistics were found.

## 8. Cards, own goals, penalty misses

### Takeaway
These are small terms. Use a shrunk player rate per 90 times expected minutes.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal: cards use a shrunk average. Own goals, penalties saved and penalties missed are not predicted —
  [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)

### Inferences
- E[cards] = −(1·P(yellow) + 3·P(red)). Note that a second yellow counts as a red.
- E[pen miss] = −2·P(takes pen)·(1 − conversion). This matters only for penalty takers.

### Gaps
- No sourced league base rates retrieved.

## 9. Simulation vs analytic EV; full distributions; correlation

### Takeaway
Analytic EV per component is enough for transfer ranking. Captaincy, risk and bonus need the full distribution,
which Monte Carlo simulation at match level gives (shared team goals, minutes, ranked BPS). FPL Review
simulates (1,000 sims for xMins), and AIrsenal sums component EVs over minutes scenarios.

### Cited Findings
- AIrsenal: E[pts] ≈ (1/|M|)·Σ_m [appearance + attacking + clean sheet − conceded + others]. Double gameweeks
  are summed — [AIrsenal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alan-turing-institute/AIrsenal/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
- FPL Review uses simulation-based xMins — [FPL Review](https://docs.fplreview.com/the-model/projections/xmins/)
- OpenFPL, an ML ensemble (RF/XGBoost by position, 122–206 features), matches FPL Review overall on mean
  prediction but outputs no distributions — [OpenFPL](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09992v1)

### Inferences
- Correlations to capture:
  - Teammates' goals and assists are linked through shared team goals. Use a multinomial allocation of each
    simulated goal to scorer and assister.
  - The goalkeeper and defenders share the same clean sheet.
  - Saves are anti-correlated with clean sheets.
  - Bonus ranks compete within a match.
- Simulate about 10^4 draws per fixture and keep the per-player points arrays. Captain EV comes from the means,
  and risk metrics (P(≥10), quantiles) come from the arrays. Squad-level variance comes from summing the
  correlated arrays.

### Gaps
- No public measure of how much captaincy decisions change when using distributions instead of means.
