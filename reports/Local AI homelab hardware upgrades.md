# Buy 24 GB, not speed, after Christmas

**Recommendation:** within £500, the upgrade that actually improves answer quality is **24 GB of model memory**. The cheapest dependable way to get it is **two used RTX 3060 12 GB cards (about £370-440) in the homelab server, fitted in January 2027 at the same time as the i5-9500 swap**. That swap lets Intel Quick Sync take over Jellyfin and frees the slot the GTX 970 now uses. 24 GB runs the 27B class of open models (Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.6-27B, Gemma 4 31B). On vendor-reported benchmarks these are a large step up from today's 9-12B models on multi-step agentic work. The tiers either side are worse buys. A 16 GB card (RTX 5060 Ti or RX 9060 XT, about £435-600) gives almost no quality gain over what the Mac mini already runs. Paying more than £500 (a used RTX 3090, a 32 GB Mac, or a Strix Halo 128 GB box) mostly buys speed, context length and headroom, not a smarter model. Hardware is only half the job: the useful part of "watch how I use the services and suggest improvements" is a **collector that turns logs and app histories into small usage summaries**. That part costs nothing, needs no downtime, can run on the current Mac mini, and can be built now while the hardware waits. Every price below is an undated listing or an aggregator snapshot from a market hit by the 2026 memory shortage. Every model score is vendor-reported unless marked otherwise.

## Quality comes from memory size: 9-12B to 27B is the jump that matters

Dan's current models already handle tool calls well. **Qwen3.5-9B scores 79.1 on TAU2-Bench and 66.1 on BFCL-V4** ([Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)), and the homelab's own bench on the M2 got the right tool and arguments 8-10 times out of 10 (local `docs/mac-mini.md`). Formatting a tool call is not the bottleneck. Depth is. Suggesting improvements from usage patterns is an open-ended, multi-step reasoning task, and that is where small models fall behind. The 27B class closes most of the gap. **Qwen3.6-27B reports 77.2 on SWE-bench Verified and 59.3 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, level with Claude 4.5 Opus's 59.3 on the second** ([Qwen3.6-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B)). **Qwen3.8-27B (August 2026) reports 73.0 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 against 78.2 for Opus 4.6 Max** ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/qwen3-8-27b-one-gpu/)). It is also the only small model on Artificial Analysis's open-model index, with a score of 34 ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source)). Long-context retrieval, which matters when reading logs, improves too: Gemma 4's 8-needle 128K score goes from **43.4 at 12B to 66.4 at 31B** ([Gemma 4 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it)).

Two caveats apply. First, as of mid-August no third party had independently scored Qwen3.8-27B ([Orcarouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/qwen-3-8-27b-benchmarks)), and vendors use different test harnesses, so comparisons across vendors are rough. Second, a large gap to frontier models remains on hard reasoning (**HLE 30.8 vs 40.0**). That argues for keeping Claude as the final reviewer, which the scout and build loop already do.

The memory tiers fall out of that. A **27B model at Q4 is about 17-19 GB, at 6-bit about 24 GB and at 8-bit about 31 GB**. Its hybrid linear attention keeps the KV cache small: about 2 GB at 32K tokens, by a community estimate that has not been verified ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/qwen3-8-27b-one-gpu/)). **24 GB therefore fits Q4 with 32K of context, and 32 GB fits Q6 with longer context.** The tier below is a trap. With 16 GB the best fits are gpt-oss-20b or Gemma 4 26B-A4B, and the latter scores **68.2 on Tau2, below Qwen3.5-9B's 79.1** ([Gemma 4 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it)). The tier above disappoints for a different reason. The 100-120B MoE models that fit in 64 GB score at or below the 27B dense models: **Qwen3.5-122B-A10B reports 72.0 SWE-bench Verified and 49.4 Terminal-Bench 2** ([card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B)), and Nemotron 3.5 Super scores 26 on the AA index against Qwen3.8's 34. Above 24-32 GB, extra memory buys a higher-precision quant, longer context and a second model kept loaded. It does not buy a smarter class of model.

Quantisation matters more for a background agent than averages suggest. A July 2026 study found **no significant change in tau2 scores at 4-bit, but tool-name hallucination rose up to 2.5x**, and a targeted repair prompt fixed it ([arXiv 2607.27275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27275)). Practical guidance is to keep the KV cache at Q8 or higher for tool calling ([insiderllm](https://insiderllm.com/guides/function-calling-local-llms/)). The cheap fix is to check tool names in the harness. Running Q6 instead of Q4 is the reason to want 32 GB later.

## Within £500, two used RTX 3060s are the only clean route to 24 GB

The 2026 memory shortage has changed the usual advice. Card prices rise most for 16 GB and 24 GB models, and Bernstein expects no relief before 2027 ([Astute Group](https://www.astutegroup.com/news/general/gpu-pricing-set-for-reset-as-ai-driven-memory-shortages-push-costs-sharply-higher/)). **A used RTX 3090 now costs about £860-1,050 in the UK** ([gpudojo](https://gpudojo.com/uk/rtx-3090)), and pcprice.watch shows a median of €1,180 across 228 eBay sales, still rising ([pcprice.watch](https://www.pcprice.watch/search/RTX3090/GB/GPU)). The classic "used 3090 for local AI" answer is now roughly double the budget.

| Option | Model memory | UK cost (indicative) | Best model tier | Downtime | Main catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2x used RTX 3060 12 GB in the server** | 24 GB | ~£370-440 | 27B at Q4 + 32K ctx | Yes (January) | PSU, slot layout and board unknown; idle ~£50-70/yr |
| RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB | 16 GB | £499 used, £485+ new | ~20B: no real gain | Yes | Can't share a host with the 970 |
| RX 9060 XT 16 GB | 16 GB | ~£435-600 new | same as above | Yes | Weakest Jellyfin encoder |
| Tesla P40 24 GB (alongside the 970) | 24 GB | ~$220-420 (US; no UK price found) | 27B at Q4 | Yes | Passive cooling, EPS power, CUDA 12 / R580 dead end |
| Server RAM to 64 GB, CPU-only | ~50 GB free | ~£230-270 (undated) | 27B fits but ~1.5-2 tok/s (est.) | Yes | Slow; shares the six cores with Jellyfin |
| 8845HS mini PC + 64 GB DDR5 | ~50 GB | ~£500-700 (unverified) | 27B at ~3-4 tok/s (est.) | None | Likely over budget; slow for dense models |
| 32 GB Mac (M2 Pro / M1 Max), sell the M2 | ~25-27 GB | net £750-1,100 at refurb prices | 27B at Q4-Q5 | None | Over budget unless found privately |

The used **RTX 3060 12 GB costs about £185-220 on eBay UK** (undated asking prices, [eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/itm/168657756901)). Two of them give 24 GB of CUDA memory, which Ollama, llama.cpp and vLLM support best, and llama.cpp splits a model's layers across two cards without trouble. Power has been estimated, not measured: about 10-15 W idle per card, or **£50-70 a year for the pair** at about £2.32 per idle watt-year. Several things are unknown, because the motherboard model and PSU have not been checked. The second slot on a 9th-gen board is probably x4 through the chipset (fine for layer splitting), two cards need roughly a 650 W supply with two 8-pin connectors (reference specs, unverified), and case length matters. New 3060s were reintroduced in 2026 and their prices **jumped about 45% in two months** ([Guru3D](https://www.guru3d.com/publish/comments/geforce-rtx-3060-12gb-prices-reportedly-jump-after-relaunch/)), so used prices could move too.

The pairing with the i5-9500 is what makes this option work. The 9400F has no integrated GPU, so today the GTX 970 handles both display and NVENC. Jellyfin ranks **Intel Quick Sync encode quality at or above NVIDIA's and says it is the easiest to set up** ([Jellyfin hardware selection](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/administration/hardware-selection)). Once the CPU is swapped, the 970 can be removed and both 3060 slots are free. This choice also leaves the driver rule intact: Ampere cards run on the 580 branch, and so can sit alongside the 970 on it. Nothing forces a driver change, and the "never install a newer branch" rule can stay until the 970 is actually gone.

The other in-budget routes each fail on something specific. The **RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB** idles at an excellent 14-17 W ([IgorsLab](https://www.igorslab.de/en/nvidia-geforce-rtx-5060-ti-16-gb-in-test-economical-consumption-surprisingly-fast-but-not-with-8gb/10)) and has an AV1 encoder. But 16 GB is the tier with no quality gain, and Blackwell needs NVIDIA's open kernel module while Maxwell needs the proprietary one, so **it cannot share the host with the 970**. The **Tesla P40** is the one 24 GB card that coexists with the 970 on R580. However, it needs a blower shroud, an EPS CPU power adapter and Above-4G decoding. It sits at **about 50 W idle with a model loaded** unless nvidia-pstated forces it down, and CUDA 13 has dropped Pascal ([computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/best-used-datacenter-gpu-local-ai/)). A homemade cooling setup running unattended 24/7 is the wrong risk for this house. **Server RAM** is the cheapest path to running a 27B model at all. The i5-9400F is limited to dual-channel DDR4-2666 ([Adorama/Intel spec](https://www.adorama.com/in684i59400f.html)), so dense 27-32B models are estimated at **1.5-2 tok/s**. Qwen3.8 thinks by default and "overthinks simple problems", so a nightly job could run for over an hour while competing with Jellyfin for CPU. **Arc** cards have the best encoder, but they have high idle draw on Linux ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/review/intel-arc-b570-linux/5)) and depend on ReBAR, which is uncertain on a Coffee Lake board.

## The Mac-side and separate-box routes avoid downtime but miss the budget

A 32 GB Mac gets the same quality tier with no change to the server. macOS lets the GPU use about 65-75% of unified memory by default, and `iogpu.wired_limit_mb` can raise that to about 26-28 GB on a 32 GB machine ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/mac-memory-pressure-local-llm); [devnote](https://github.com/ivanopcode/devnote-override-macos-metal-vram-cap)). That is enough for a 27B model at Q4-Q5. The best-value machine is a **Mac Studio M1 Max 32 GB at about £1,175 (Amazon Renewed)** ([refurb.me](https://www.refurb.me/refurbished/mac-studio/with-m1-max)). Its 400 GB/s memory bandwidth is double an M2 Pro's (llama.cpp's 7B Q4_0 table gives M1 Max 61.2 tok/s, M2 Pro 38.9 and M2 21.9, per [llama.cpp #4167](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/4167)), and it idles at about 11 W ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102027)). The **M2 Pro 32 GB mini starts at £1,199** refurbished ([refurb.me](https://www.refurb.me/gb/refurbished/mac-mini/sku/MNH73-134)). The current M2 16 GB sells for roughly £370-400 (listings, [refurb.me](https://www.refurb.me/gb/stats/mac-mini/mac-mini-m2-early-2023)), which puts the **net cost at about £780-850, well over budget**. A private sale £150-300 cheaper would bring it to roughly £500-650. That saving is an assumption, not a verified figure. Avoid the M4 Pro 24 GB: only about 16-18 GB of it is usable for models. Clustering the old M2 with a new Mac is not worth it either: llama.cpp RPC slowed **from 20.4 to 15.2 tok/s going from one node to four** in Jeff Geerling's test ([lobsterpack](https://www.lobsterpack.com/blog/exo-distributed-ai-cluster-macs/)).

**Clustering two M2 16 GB minis (added after review).** Pairing the current M2 with a second one over a Thunderbolt 4 cable is the one case where clustering suits this use. The aim is to pool memory so a bigger model fits, not to gain speed. With the GPU limit raised to about 12-13 GB per Mac, the pair has roughly 24-26 GB: enough for a 27B model at Q4 with 32K of context, with no server downtime and only a few watts of idle draw. It is slow, because the model is split by layers and the two Macs take turns on each token; the estimate is under 5 tok/s for 27B. RDMA, which makes exo fast, needs Thunderbolt 5, which the M2 doesn't have. llama.cpp RPC has no authentication, so it must listen only on the Thunderbolt link. Both Macs have to stay up, and Hermes loses its dedicated model. **Price rules it out:** in October 2026 CeX lists the M2 16 GB at about £700, and the £450-500 units are 8 GB. An 8 GB Mac adds only about 5 GB of usable GPU memory, so the pair would total about 17-18 GB, not enough for 27B. Dan paid about £350 for his M2 16 GB a year ago. The route works only with a 16 GB unit from eBay or a private seller for about £450 or less. The same price rise means the current M2 may resell for more than the £370-400 assumed above, which lowers the net cost of a 32 GB Mac. CeX's trade-in price was not checked.

A separate **Ryzen 8845HS mini PC with 64 GB DDR5** avoids server work entirely and keeps the M2 running Hermes. But the shortage has made 64 GB of SO-DIMM expensive, so the estimated total of £500-700 is unverified. At about 90 GB/s it would run dense 27B models at an estimated 3-4 tok/s. **Strix Halo 128 GB** boxes are the real "separate box" upgrade, with up to 96 GB available to the GPU. UK prices conflict badly: TechRadar listed the Bosgame M5 at £1,103-1,259 ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/bosgame-m5-ai-mini-pc-review)), while German deals later reached €2,589 ([mydealz](https://www.mydealz.de/deals/ryzen-ai-max-395-mini-pc-mit-128gb-ram-2tb-ssd-fur-ki-und-gaming-2655372)).

**What the step above £500 buys.** About £850-1,100 gets a used RTX 3090: the same 24 GB tier as two 3060s, in one card, with more bandwidth. It also needs a 750 W+ supply and can idle at 100 W+ on Linux until PowerMizer is set to Adaptive ([NVIDIA forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/bug-report-idle-power-draw-is-astronomical-with-rtx-3090/155632)). A net £750-1,100 gets a 32 GB Mac with no downtime and room for Q6. Roughly £1,100-1,300+ before selling the M2 gets Strix Halo, which can run gpt-oss-120b, keep 27B at Q8 with long context, and hold several models loaded at once. None of these, on current vendor numbers, reaches a model class above the 27B dense models. **Above £500, Dan pays for speed, precision and convenience, not intelligence.** The one exception is the no-downtime Mac route, which matters only if waiting until January is unacceptable.

## The software pipeline matters more than the card, and can start now

What people actually run is a scheduled pipeline: collect, filter with plain code, summarise with a local model, send to a human. The best-documented homelab example runs on a 30-minute cron job. Substring filters remove **60-70% of log volume** before Qwen 7-14B sees it ([dev.to/pdegidio](https://dev.to/pdegidio/i-built-an-ai-log-monitor-for-my-homelab-local-llm-reads-my-arr-logs-so-i-dont-have-to-503n)). An XDA write-up found that tool-using agents misfire when tools have similar names ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/gave-local-llm-control-homelab-nothing-touched-cloud/)). This homelab's logs make the point. One day was **10.2 MB across 26 containers, 73% of it from the socket-proxy access log**. With that excluded and numbers masked, it reduces to **1,094 unique templates (about 67K tokens), and just 73 warning/error templates (about 4.5K tokens)** (local measurement, 25 Oct 2026). Usage signals are smaller still: Traefik per-router hourly counts, Jellyfin playback history, Seerr requests, arr grabs and dashboard tile clicks come to a few KB a day. "Always-on" should mean collecting all the time on the server and reasoning once a night or once a week, and at that cadence speed does not matter.

The research supports a split: **the local model compresses, Claude judges**. Model cascades match large-model quality at about 40% of the cost when the escalation signal is good ([arXiv 2509.21837](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21837v3)). The loki-log-summary project does exactly this: local Ollama for per-window summaries, Claude for the scheduled fleet report, and redaction done in code ([GitHub](https://github.com/jdbway/loki-log-summary)). For Dan, the concrete build is:

1. A collector with no LLM, reading through the socket proxy and app APIs.
2. Traefik access logging, which is not enabled now: Traefik logged only 16 lines that day.
3. A nightly local digest that outputs structured JSON findings.
4. Those findings feed the Friday scout, which writes to `ideas/proposed/` for Dan's approval.

Lightweight metrics can come from Beszel, at about 10-80 MB ([selfhosting.sh](https://selfhosting.sh/compare/grafana-vs-beszel/)). Loki is optional: one anecdote saw a query push it to 4 GB+ ([Grafana community](https://community.grafana.com/t/loki-monolithic-memory-consumption/112032)). Two settings matter. Ollama's default context window is small, so set `num_ctx` explicitly ([virtua.cloud](https://www.virtua.cloud/learn/en/tutorials/ai-log-analysis-ollama-vps)). Keep the KV cache at Q8. Log lines can be written by outsiders (user-agents, release names), so the watcher must hold no tools that take action. Red Hat's MCP guidance says to treat all fetched text as possibly malicious ([Red Hat](https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/mcp-security-logging-and-runtime-security-measures)). The existing read-only proxy, guard hook and Proposed-tab approval already follow that.

## Conclusion

The question changes once the numbers are in. With Claude doing the final judgement, a 9-12B model on the current Mac mini can extract and summarise usage well enough. A 24 GB card earns its place when Dan wants the local model to write the proposals itself, or to diagnose across many steps without sending data to the cloud. So the right order is to build the collector and digest now at no cost, run them through November and December on the M2, and see whether local quality is actually the weak link. If it is, buy two 3060s in January alongside the i5-9500.

Before buying, check three things without downtime: the board model (from `/sys/class/dmi/id/board_name`), the PSU's wattage and its 8-pin connectors, and the slot spacing and case length. If any of them rules out two cards, the fallback is a privately bought 32 GB Mac rather than a 16 GB GPU. Keep the M2 running Hermes either way, as a separate Ollama endpoint for fast, small tool-calling models.
